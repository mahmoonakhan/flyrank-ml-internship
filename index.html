Prioritizing High-Decay Search Pages: A Grouped-Validation Content Action Engine

Track: Machine Learning Capstone | Lane: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring

Title & Abstract

Managing enterprise organic search performance requires rapid identification of high-value pages undergoing performance decay. In this study, we frame page decay as a grouped ranking and classification problem across anonymized client domains. We evaluate traditional rule-based baselines against Random Forest and Gradient Boosting architectures using a strict 5-fold grouped cross-validation strategy. Our ensemble model achieves an out-of-fold ROC-AUC of 0.784, outperforming the baseline by +0.076 while guarding against cross-client data leakage. These validated predictions are compiled into an operational content action playbook with concrete human-in-the-loop review guardrails.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Content marketing and SEO teams frequently struggle with resource allocation, often rewriting healthy pages or ignoring critical decay on high-traffic assets. This project designs a decision-support system that identifies high-impression pages experiencing steady multi-week search visibility decay. The core action enabled is an automated, prioritized rewrite queue that minimizes wasted copywriting hours while preserving organic acquisition channels.

2. Data Contract & Public-Safe Scope

The dataset represents multi-client search performance metrics provided via the FlyRank research repository. To guarantee data privacy and compliance with public-safe rules:

  • All URLs, domain names, client identifiers, and raw search queries are stripped and anonymized into surrogate keys (page_id, client_id).
  • Features include historical impressions, clicks, average position, days since last refresh, and computed Click-Through Rate (CTR).
  • No future-window metrics or target-leaking artifacts were included in feature matrices.

3. Methodology & Validation Design

To evaluate performance without artificial overfitting:

  • Leakage Prevention: Standard random K-fold splits allow intra-domain memorization. We implemented strict GroupKFold (5 splits) grouped by client_id to evaluate true out-of-domain generalization.
  • Baseline Formulation: Week 4 additive heuristic score combining normalized impression scale and negative growth rates.
  • Candidate Estimators: Logistic Regression (Linear Benchmark), Random Forest Classifier, and Gradient Boosting.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

In accordance with honest research principles, our claims are bounded to observed, measured, and directional decision support:

  • No Causal Proof: The model observes correlation between page staleness and click declines; it cannot prove Google algorithm causation or competitor behavior.
  • Not "Predicting Google": The system does not forecast search engine core updates or guarantee post-refresh traffic recovery.

6. Ranked Recommendations (Content Action Playbook)

Model scores are translated into clear reason codes and operational actions:

  • HIGH_VOLUME_DECAY → ACTION_REFRESH_CONTENT: Requires editorial overhaul and schema refresh.
  • CTR_OPPORTUNITY → ACTION_METADATA_SNIPPET_FIX: Requires title tag and meta snippet rewrites.
  • No-Go Boundary: Automated content replacement and auto-redirects are strictly prohibited; mandatory human verification is enforced for all top-decile recommendations.
