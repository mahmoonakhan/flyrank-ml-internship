Prioritizing High-Decay Search Pages: A Grouped-Validation Content Action Engine

Author: Mahmoona Khan • Track: Machine Learning Capstone • Lane: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring

Title & Abstract

Managing enterprise search performance requires rapidly identifying high-value pages undergoing organic decay. In this research, we formulate page decay prioritization as a machine learning ranking task evaluated across anonymized multi-client search performance logs. We implement strict 5-fold grouped cross-validation by client identifier to eliminate cross-domain data leakage and test true generalizability. Our trained Random Forest ensemble achieves an out-of-fold ROC-AUC of 0.7844, outperforming the heuristic baseline by +0.076 on identical evaluation folds. These predictions feed directly into an automated content action playbook that assigns explicit reason codes and preserves human editorial sign-off for critical interventions.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Content marketing and SEO teams frequently struggle to allocate scarce editorial resources, often spending hundreds of hours manually reviewing web pages or revising healthy URLs while failing to spot critical traffic drops on core revenue drivers. This project builds a production-ready decision-support system that flags pages experiencing persistent search impression and click decay. The primary objective is to output a ranked triage queue with actionable reason codes, enabling editorial teams to prioritize high-leverage content refreshes while preventing wasted copywriting spend.

2. Data Contract & Public-Safe Scope

The analysis is conducted on the multi-client FlyRank search warehouse data release. To satisfy all data privacy and public-safety requirements:

  • All sensitive fields—including raw search query strings, destination URLs, and client company names—have been sanitized and replaced with anonymized surrogate identifiers (page_id, client_id).
  • Features are aggregated over historical 30-day pre-decision windows ($T \le 0$), including total impressions, clicks, average position, days since last refresh, and derived CTR.
  • The mid-panel month 2026-03 was used for feature engineering and training, retaining the final month (2026-06) as a sealed evaluation partition.
  • All future-window performance data and target-leaking signals were explicitly excluded from the feature space.

3. Methodology & Validation Design

Standard random splits cause significant data leakage in search data because pages belonging to the same client share domain authority and URL structures. To enforce rigorous scientific standards:

  • Validation Design: We utilize a strict GroupKFold (5 splits) grouped by client_id to evaluate model generalization on unseen client domains.
  • Baseline Heuristic (W04): An additive scoring formula combining normalized historical impressions and negative click growth rates:
    score = (norm_impressions * 0.50) + (max(0, -growth_rate) * 0.50)
  • Evaluated Estimators: Logistic Regression (linear baseline), Random Forest Classifier, and Gradient Boosting.
  • Label Formulation: A binary target indicating high-traffic pages (impressions > median) suffering significant velocity drop (growth rate < -0.10).

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

Safe Claim Boundaries: In compliance with research guidelines, all findings are framed strictly as measured, directional decision support.

  • No Causal Claims: This model measures empirical correlation between staleness, ranking trends, and traffic drops; it does not claim to prove the underlying causes of Google ranking updates.
  • No "Predicting Google": The system does not forecast search engine core algorithm changes or guarantee traffic recovery post-refresh.
  • Domain Representation: Performance metrics reflect aggregate historical patterns and may vary on niche websites with non-standard search patterns.

6. Ranked Recommendations (Content Action Playbook)

Raw prediction probabilities are mapped to actionable editorial categories:

  • HIGH_VOLUME_DECAY → ACTION_REFRESH_CONTENT: Major content and schema rewrite for high-traffic assets losing momentum.
  • CTR_OPPORTUNITY → ACTION_METADATA_SNIPPET_FIX: Title tag and meta description optimization for top-ranking pages suffering below-average click-through rates.
  • SHARP_TRAFFIC_DROP → ACTION_INTERNAL_LINK_BOOST: Internal link reinforcement and technical canonical verification.
  • No-Go Boundary: Automated content re-publishing and URL redirects are strictly forbidden; human review is required before modifying live client assets.
